# Audience

The site doesn't publish a formal ICP. This separates what it says from what I inferred, mostly from the job titles in about 90 reviews.

## Stated

- **Overall:** non-technical teams; "operators, not engineers"
- **Certification:** marketers, operators, and founders, plus people switching from ChatGPT or Gemini
- **Bootcamp:** business owners and independent experts, scale-up operators who own pipeline, and career changers
- **Masterclass:** freelancers, founders, operators, and marketers already using Claude

## Inferred segments

### 1. Company buyers of team training

- **Likely people:** a founder, CEO, or functional leader, or an AI enablement, L&D, or IT lead
- **Situation:** the company already pays for AI licenses, but people aren't using them in daily work
- **Wanted result:** real usage inside existing workflows. The Product Marketing Alliance case is framed around a 10x rise in Claude usage.
- **Evidence:** the homepage's main call to action and its "AI gap" section

### 2. Corporate individual contributors and managers

- **Examples in reviews:** product managers, marketing managers, account executives, consultants, and program managers at companies such as Sage, Instacart, Gartner, Accenture, and ServiceNow
- **Situation:** they want practical skills and can often expense the course
- **Why they matter:** they may be a route into team training. This is my guess, as the site doesn't say it.

### 3. Founders, solo operators, and small business owners

- **Examples in reviews:** founders, fractional CFOs, consultants, and agency owners
- **Situation:** they have small teams or none, and use AI to get more done
- **Wanted result:** time back, and sometimes less need to hire. One founder said the course made them question whether they needed an assistant.
- **Why they matter:** they appear very often in reviews and are probably a large share of course revenue, but the site has no data to confirm that.

### 4. Growth and GTM operators

- **Examples in reviews:** growth specialists, marketing directors, RevOps, and GTM roles
- **Situation:** they own pipeline and want working systems, not theory
- **Wanted result:** automated outbound, inbound, and signal monitoring
- **Main offer:** the bootcamp

### 5. Career changers

- **Evidence:** named as a bootcamp audience, with a GitHub portfolio as a course outcome. A few reviewers are between roles.

## Recurring problems in their own words

Paraphrased from reviews and homepage copy:

- They use only a fraction of what Claude can do and don't know what they're missing.
- Learning alone from videos is slow, and the tools change too quickly to keep up.
- They have tools and ideas, but no consistent workflows.
- They need to apply it to their own job, not learn theory they must translate later.
- They worry that AI training is hype or out of date.
- They don't have a technical background and fear being lost.

## Who it isn't for

- Developers, or anyone looking for an engineering course. The certification and masterclass don't cover Claude Code.
- People who have no paid AI plan and don't intend to get one
- People who want theory or strategy without building anything (inferred)
