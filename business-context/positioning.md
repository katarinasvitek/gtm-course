# Positioning and messaging

## Core argument

Companies have already bought AI tools, and what's missing is the skill to use them. NotLinear closes that gap by teaching AI through the workflows a team already runs.

## The problem as the site frames it

1. **Licenses aren't skills.** Teams have ChatGPT, Claude, or Copilot, and adoption still stalls.
2. **Use cases are the bottleneck.** The hard part is applying AI to daily work, not the technology.
3. **Nobody inside bridges the gap.** Operators know the work and IT knows the systems, but few people have both domain knowledge and AI fluency.

## Differentiators

| Differentiator | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Built for non-technical people | Audience definition, course scope, reviews from non-technical buyers |
| Practical over theoretical | 60–80% hands-on courses; reviews repeatedly praise this |
| Taught by an operator | The "operator first, trainer second" bio and the $0 to $1M claim |
| Up to date | Reviewers note that content keeps pace with fast-changing tools |
| Mapped to the team's workflows | How the team training is described |
| Personal access to the instructor | 1:1 sessions, office hours, reviews about his generosity with time |
| Low-risk trial | Free workshops ("try before you buy") and the Maven Guarantee |

## Taglines

- "Grow non linearly."
- "Operators, not engineers."
- "AI training for non-technical teams"
- "Try before you buy."

## Voice

- **Direct and plain:** short, declarative sentences
- **Contrast-driven:** often says what something isn't before what it is
- **Anti-hype:** "no fluff" framing and little AI jargon on the homepage
- **Confident but practical:** claims lean on numbers and named customers
- **Personal:** Pietro is named throughout, and "we" is used for the company

## Calls to action

1. **Book a call** for teams (the main action, repeated throughout)
2. **See our courses** for individuals
3. **See the next dates** for free workshops, recommended to anyone unsure where to start
4. **Subscribe** to the newsletter

## Competitive picture

The site names no competitors. **Inferred**, the alternatives a buyer would weigh are:

- Doing nothing, or relying on licenses and self-teaching
- Free videos and vendor training
- Other cohort courses on Maven
- Large consultancies and corporate L&D providers
- Building an internal AI champion program

I haven't researched any of these. Competitor research would be needed before using this list in messaging.
